# Time-resolved drive integration

The new frozen simulation profile must opt in with `ENERGY.version='fixed_pack_joint_dense_v2'`, `integration_method='dense_midpoint_v1'`, `integration_substeps=32`, `comparison_substeps=16`, and `minimum_soc=BATTERY.terminal_margin`. The battery configuration declares `streaming_estimator_version='fixed_pack_resolved_ekf_v3'`; the old single-interval `advance` entrypoint rejects this version to prevent an accidental cadence change. Historical configurations without `integration_method` continue to use `loadStep` and the historical interval-mean speed calculation.

The production ODE is still integrated once, with its original two-point time span and tolerances. The dense solution provides the 32 equal-duration midpoint loads. Their mean power determines interval energy. The 16-grid midpoints and both endpoints carry no quadrature weight and are included in sampled drive checks. The electrical mapping uses the actual analytic derivative of the repository IK, including the exact hip expression when the integrated rotation matrix has small orthogonality error.

Each fine midpoint power is applied for two battery halfsteps. The existing physical high-voltage quadratic root and 3RC update are reused without a new estimator update. Midpoint, comparison-grid and endpoint circuit solves have zero duration and book no energy. Drive qualification uses the minimum sampled loaded bus voltage minus the maximum sampled required motor voltage within each MPC interval. This conservative noncoincident envelope is deliberately stronger than checking only matching times. It remains a sampled check, not a certified continuous-time maximum. The true configured SOC reserve is checked after every physical halfstep; a failure rejects the entire MPC interval using the established transaction rollback.

For the EKF, each measured halfstep cell current is passed to the exact local predictor, and its transition Jacobian is composed as `F_total = F_j * F_total`. No process noise or voltage update is added between substeps. At the original MPC interval end, `C = F_total*C_previous*F_total' + Q`; thus the original 100 Hz process covariance retains its stated discrete cadence. The final voltage observation is instantaneous: `h(z)=OCV(z_SOC)-R0(z_SOC)*I_end-sum(z_RC)`, with `H=[dOCV-dR0*I_end,-1,-1,-1]`. One Joseph-form covariance update follows. Ground-truth SOC never enters the estimator prediction or voltage equation.

Scalar current logs contain charge-equivalent interval means; power logs contain interval mean power. Voltage and posterior SOC are endpoint values, so `mean(P)` need not equal `V_endpoint*mean(I)`. Joint arrays are all taken at the single sample with the largest required voltage; separate scalar motor torque and voltage fields retain sampled maxima. `drive_min_pack_voltage_V`, the conservative headroom field, integration version/grid, comparison energy and precise sample time are logged. Optional drive captures retain the exact ODE inputs for deterministic reconstruction.

The original batch battery crosscheck is pinned explicitly to the historical 4S6P interval-mean provider. Its one-current-per-interval assumption cannot independently reproduce the new substep trajectory. The new runtime tests separately verify conservation, the composed estimator Jacobian by finite differences, exact-truth estimator propagation, endpoint zero-duration accounting, true-SOC reserve handling, both MPC consumer paths, unchanged mechanical endpoints and transactional failure rollback.
